RSVP AND LIVING ARTWORK REVISION
RSVP now follows the celebration stack; guest FAQs appear last. The form is
an arched ivory stationery card set against the emerald Pichwai garden, with
antique-gold rules, a lotus motif and handwritten salutation. The desktop
composition pairs the card with a monogram invitation panel; mobile stacks
the invitation and form. The preview dialog uses the same palette.

A fixed gold RSVP button remains available above the form, including on mobile
above the bottom navigation. It links directly to the form and hides when the
form reaches the viewport, while the reply dialog is open, and on the opening
screen. It reappears when guests scroll back above the form. The header and
bottom navigation also retain their RSVP links.

Haldi has drifting petals; Sangeet has warm lamp glows and gold motes;
the wedding painting has a breathing fire glow and rising embers. The new
resort gouache painting has lamp glows and pool ripples. Subtle image motion
complements the effects. A shared animation loop is capped at 30 fps; canvas
resolution is capped at 1.5 device pixels per CSS pixel. Only visible scenes
render. Artwork can be paused, stops in hidden browser tabs, and respects
system reduced-motion preferences, including changes while the site is open.

The opening and cover retain their design; names and monograms now place
Priyanka before Shiva throughout the invitation. Confirmed timings
and the WhatsApp RSVP recipient are preserved. No RSVP has been sent.


ANIMATED FLOWERS AND PHOTO STACK
The original Pichwai paintings now move on both the seal-opening screen and
the hero cover. A light WebGL displacement bends the floral vines and gives
individual lotus blossoms a subtle breathing motion. Each side has its own
phase; typography and invitation controls stay in their original DOM positions.
The opening doors carry the animated painting with them. A discreet Pause
flowers control shares its state with the existing artwork pause control.

The garden caps rendering at 30 fps and pixel density at 1.5. Only visible
scenes run. Reduced-motion preferences and hidden tabs stop rendering; failed
GPU initialization leaves the original CSS image visible. Images are shared
between scenes and selected to match the portrait/landscape CSS artwork.

Ceremony cards now pin while the next one slides above them, settling with a
slight alternating tilt, scale and soft shadow. On a tall mobile card, the
sticky top offset is negative so its full contents scroll into view before it
pins. The bottom navigation has a dedicated clearance. Card heights are
remeasured after font loading and resizing. Keyboard focus on a covered card
returns it to view. Reduced motion restores a regular vertical list.

Validation includes DOM interaction checks, mocked rendering lifecycle checks,
and phone/desktop card-position calculations. Browser rendering remains
unverified because preview browser launch is blocked in this environment.


CALENDAR AFTER RSVP
The ceremony cards and countdown no longer have calendar buttons. The RSVP
review dialog offers a single calendar keepsake after the reply actions, only
for guests who select attending. The date and button label reflect their
reviewed selections, and declining hides and disables the option.

Haldi and/or Sangeet reserve 25 January 2027. Wedding reserves 26 January.
Choosing celebrations across both dates reserves 25–26 January. One calendar
file contains a single all-day event, with the selected ceremony times and
venue in its notes. It uses the reviewed RSVP snapshot; reviewing an edited
reply updates the reservation. The website does not claim the RSVP was sent
or the calendar import completed.

https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc5545#section-3.6.1

snapshot behavior and declined/invalid replies passed DOM and logic checks.
